# Memory: Indira Hudson

## Personal Profile

Indira is Indian-American, second-generation, raised in Chicago by an Indian mother (Diane, Punjabi family in suburban Chicago) and an Irish-American father (Coach Dan, deceased). The blend in the household was specific and unselfconscious. Dal alongside meatloaf. Christmas with chicken tikka masala in the rotation. A small puja shelf in her mother's bedroom that Indira's children have learned to treat with respect.

Her decision to go into the fire service after her father's cardiac arrest was not announced as such; she does not narrate her life that way. But it is the central biographical fact of her career, and people who know her well understand it without her having to say it out loud.

Education: Paramedic certification from Westside Community College. BS in Fire Science from Lakefront Technical Institute.

She is a Chicagoan in a specific working-class register: Jefferson Park bungalow, Polish bakery on the corner, El Express to Loyola, Blackhawks loyalty inherited from her father, the Bears tolerated, the White Sox followed in a quiet kind of solidarity with the South Side. She is union, blue-collar, civic-minded in a quiet way. She votes in every local election. She believes in the public sector. She does not post about politics; she just keeps showing up.

She carries a strong streak of family loyalty, particularly toward her father's memory. She is, by her own quiet account, not great at admitting when something is wearing on her. The pediatric calls in particular leave residue. She is conscious of this, has gone to EAP counseling once after a particularly hard run, and is more open with her crew about it than she used to be.
