# Memory: Larry Bates

## Personal Profile

Larry is the third generation of a brewing family in the southern Appalachian mountains. The Bates family has been in these mountains for four generations and brewing for three. He moves through a day with the steadiness of someone whose work is governed by processes that take weeks rather than minutes. The calm is what twenty years of mash rest, fermentation crawl, and barrel age settle into. He is decisive when needed and slow when he can afford to be, does not change recipes on impulse, and is loyal in ways that occasionally cost him commercially.

**Education**: B.Sc. Fermentation Science, Oregon State University, 2009. Apprenticed under his father, Thomas, for six years before assuming head brewer in 2015. Siebel Institute advanced brewing technology, 2014.

**Languages**: English (native), German (conversational, improving for export markets), basic French from a 2018 Quebec craft exchange.

**Citizenship**: U.S. citizen, born in Asheville. Lived in Corvallis through university.

**Cultural identity**: Appalachian by inheritance and conviction. Soft Western North Carolina accent. Family is Protestant in the loose Appalachian way; church attendance more cultural than devotional. The brewhouse is closer to his sacred space than any church.

**Philosophy**: Believes beer is a conversation with grain, and that brewing is stewardship rather than manufacturing. Believes in continuity over disruption and thinks about the next generation more than quarter-to-quarter results. Honours inherited tradition by adding to it carefully rather than copying it exactly.

## Work & Projects

**Role**: Head brewer and owner of Bates Brewing Company. Responsible for every brewing decision: recipe design, grain selection, mash management, fermentation management, conditioning, blending. Also runs business operations: distribution, finances, marketing (with Sarah), regulatory compliance, brewery maintenance.

**Staff**: 5 seasonal brewers (November through March), 2 year-round (Greg plus one).

**Production**: Approximately 2,500 barrels annually. Product line: barrel-aged imperial stout (flagship, 14 months aging), farmhouse ale (volume seller), pale ale, and seasonal limited releases. Locally grown barley, Appalachian hops, mountain spring water, in-house cultivated yeast, proprietary fermentation blends.

**Key projects**:

- **2026 brewing season wrap-up**: 14 batches in February 2026, all successful. Experimental barrel-aged imperial stout flagged for competition. Currently in post-season conditioning, blending, and bottling.
- **Export market expansion**: Working with British Craft Imports (UK) and Pacific Craft Distributors (Singapore) to grow exports from 10% to 20% of production. British Craft Imports has requested a 20% volume increase; Pacific Craft is asking for an exclusive barrel-aged allocation. Target: new UK pub partnerships by October 2026.
- **Brewery renovation Phase 2**: Fermentation room climate control plus tank temperature monitoring. Phase 1 (cold storage) completed 2025. Phase 2 budget: $350,000. Contractor: Blue Ridge Construction. Window: August through September 2026, must complete before November brewing season.
- **2027 experimental expression**: Barrel-aged imperial stout using 100% locally malted barley at an extended 18-month aging.

**Professional development**: Entering the 2026 Great American Beer Festival and the World Beer Cup. Considering a Belgian study trip in autumn 2027 (monasteries, Wallonia farmhouse breweries, Brussels bars).

## Interests & Hobbies

- **Brewing-adjacent reading**: A small library of out-of-print brewing texts treated with the reverence other people give to first editions. Currently two years into a Belgian monastic brewing tradition that generates an experiment every paragraph.
- **Beer journaling**: Handwritten notes on every batch since the apprentice years. A shelf of journals above the brewhouse office desk. He can pull a 2014 journal and tell you the third-day fermentation temperature of the batch he is comparing against.
- **Walking with Hana**: Saturday mornings, regular route, regular bakery stop. The most carefully protected hour of his week.
- **Hiking the Blue Ridge**: Off-season autumn weekends, alone or with Sarah. Has worked out more brewing problems on a ridge trail than at a desk.
- **Hot springs**: Western North Carolina mineral springs as recovery practice, monthly. Regional inheritance more than hobby.

## Home & Living

- **Home**: Traditional farmhouse attached to the brewery compound. Five bedrooms (three in the main house with open-plan living and a 2018-renovated kitchen, plus a separate wing for Thomas and Margaret). Small garden. Stone path to the brewhouse.
- **Aesthetic**: Brewhouse-functional. Copper, wood, stone, the smell of yeast, the soft late-afternoon light through high windows. Resisted modernisation beyond what production reliability requires. The new climate control system is the largest aesthetic compromise.
- **Family farmhouse**: Comfortable rather than designed. Sarah handles most decorating. Bookshelves functional. The kitchen is the room everyone gathers in. Walls hold grandparents in front of the brewery, beer-festival ribbons in shadow boxes, and a watercolour Hana painted that Larry refuses to take down.
- **Home projects**: Kitchen ventilation upgrade, Hana's room redecoration (reading corner), garden path repair (frost damage).
- **Personal style**: Work shirts and jeans most days. The same boots he has owned for seven years. One good flannel for cold mornings. One wool coat for outdoor industry events. One suit, bought for an industry award ceremony, that has come out twice.

## Preferences

- **News**: *Wall Street Journal* (business daily), *Asheville Citizen-Times* (local), Brewers Association newsletter, *Craft Beer & Brewing*. Focused reader, does not browse aimlessly.
- **Music**: Classic rock (Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd), blues, Americana, bluegrass. Silence during critical brewing operations.
- **Podcasts**: *Craft Beer Talk*, *NPR Morning Edition*, *Fermentation Lab*, *Business Today*. Drives and off-season mornings.
- **Reading**: Brewing science texts (Ray Daniels, John Palmer). American literature: McCarthy, Hemingway, Steinbeck. History of American craftsmanship.
- **Gifts received**: Artisan ceramics, quality knives, woodworking tools, rare beers from other breweries.
- **Gifts given**: His own beer (always; the most personal gift he can offer) and Appalachian specialty foods. A specific bottle for an old mentor, a specific cookbook for Margaret. For Sarah's birthday he has slowly learned to do flowers.
- **Food**: American home cooking, seasonal emphasis. Margaret cooks weekdays; Sarah weekends. Locally milled bread, hearty soups, grilled meats, brewing-season stews, Margaret's pickled vegetables. Regular spots: The Farmhouse Tavern (monthly with Moreland), Blue Ridge Smokehouse (celebrations), Craft Beer Hall (industry networking).
- **Drinks**: His own beer daily with dinner in a small amount. Competitor products tasted regularly as professional practice. Drip coffee throughout the day. Occasional bourbon from a local craft distillery.
- **Sensory anchors**: Smell-anchored. Warm mash, yeast in active fermentation, petrichor on the Asheville hills, cold November air. Sounds: slow bubble of early fermentation, Hana's footsteps on the brewery floor, glycol chiller hum, brewhouse Sunday silence.
- **Comfort and decompression**: The brewhouse stool near the lautering tun in the late-afternoon light. When the brewhouse is not the answer, a short ridge hike or a long walk through the back fields with snow on them.
- **Shopping**: Carefully and with a long horizon. Pays full price for brewing equipment, proper boots, and the few books he buys new. Refuses full price on almost anything else. Sends his parents small amounts when he can. Gives to a local watershed protection group, the Brewers Association educational fund, and an Asheville music venue.
- **Travel**: Mostly industry. Leisure once a year, off-season, with Sarah and Hana, to somewhere quieter than Asheville. Prefers driving to flying for any reachable destination. Rented cabins in mountain towns. Coastal North Carolina in October.
- **Dislikes**: Breweries that confuse marketing for craft. Consultants who use "consumer insights" without irony. Distributors who pressure for volume at the expense of quality. Over-hopped beer that buries the malt. Loud restaurants. Cell phones at the dinner table.
