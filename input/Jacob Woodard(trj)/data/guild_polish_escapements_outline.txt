GUILD QUARTERLY — TALK OUTLINE
Polish longcase escapements, 18th and 19th century
Florianska, 21 October evening session, ~25 min plus Q&A

I. Opening
   - The myth of a single "Polish school" — there isn't one
   - Workshop geography: Gdańsk, Warszawa, Wilno, Kraków, regional shops
   - What we actually inherit: imports, copies, and home-grown improvisation

II. The verge survives later than the textbooks say
   - Local conservatism: rural shops fitting verge well into the early 19th century
   - Why? Spare parts economy, journeyman training, client expectation
   - One bench observation: verge wear patterns in Polish iron vs Swiss brass

III. Anchor and the slow Polish convert
   - Anchor adoption lagged English shops by half a century
   - Material story: domestic steel quality, hardening practices
   - Field example: the Pomeranian longcases at the Toruń museum

IV. Deadbeat — the Warsaw exception
   - 1830s Warsaw shops experimenting earlier than the regional average
   - Foreign journeymen, French training, French escape wheels
   - Why this matters for dating unsigned movements

V. Restoration ethics — quick word
   - Replace like with like, document everything, never invent a maker
   - The "improved escapement" temptation is a trap
   - Reversibility above all

VI. Q&A — expect the usual: signed vs unsigned attribution
