# Memory: Jacob Woodard

## Personal Profile

Jacob is an American watchmaker from Philadelphia who has built his adult life in Kraków. He left the United States in 1988 for an apprenticeship in Le Locle, Switzerland, where four years of advanced restoration training under German-speaking masters shaped the way he holds a tool and the way he speaks about quality. In 1994 he moved to Kraków and opened Woodard Horology on ulica Józefa, choosing Poland on its own terms rather than as a stopover. The choice still feels right on most days. The handful when it does not, he keeps to himself.

**Education and credentials**: Associate degree in Fine Mechanics from Philadelphia Community College of Technology (1986). Advanced restoration training in Le Locle, Switzerland (1988 to 1992). Master Craftsman certification from the Polish Chamber of Crafts (1998).

**Languages**: English (native), Polish (fluent, living in Poland since 1994), German (fluent, trained in Switzerland), basic French for horology terminology. He shifts between languages without commentary.

**Citizenship and residency**: US citizen with a Polish permanent residency card on its second renewal. He has not seriously considered moving back.

**Temperament**: Measured, attentive, slower-paced than the street outside the workshop. He listens longer than he speaks. When he speaks, sentences are short, precise, and surprisingly warm under the precision. The Philadelphia inflection in his English is faint after three decades. The Polish carries an American softness that locals still notice.

**Philosophy**: Do the careful work, keep your word, honor the tradition that taught you, train the next generation patiently, and accept that some things must take the time they take. Suspicious of speed as a value in itself. The fastest restoration is rarely the best.

**Faith**: Quiet, durable Catholicism. The Sunday Mass at St. Catherine's, the maintenance of the church clock that Father Newman trusts him with, the small daily habits of grace before meals. Not a performance, the rhythm of his interior life.

**American identity**: Held with affection rather than nostalgia. The Fourth of July BBQ, the Thanksgiving dinner he has hosted for two decades, NPR podcasts in the workshop, the Hemingway and Steinbeck on his shelf. These are the threads he keeps.

**Workshop founding**: Established Woodard Horology in 1994. The building was paid off in 2019, and the workshop has carried no credit since 1998.

## Work & Projects

**Woodard Horology**: Independent watchmaking and clock restoration workshop in Kazimierz, Kraków. Ground floor of a historic building on ulica Józefa. Founded by Jacob in 1994, now in its thirty-second year. Open Tuesday to Saturday; Monday is admin and sourcing; closed Sundays.

**Specialization**: Restoration and repair of antique timepieces. Pocket watches, mantel clocks, longcase clocks, and tower clocks. Known for restoration of 18th and 19th-century Polish and German movements. Also services modern mechanical watches.

**Active commissions**:
- **Krasicki Estate Clock**: 1820s Polish longcase from a private estate near Przemyśl. Requires a hand-forged escape wheel ordered through Stefan Müller, approximate cost PLN 2,800. Four-month timeline. High-value.
- **St. Catherine's Tower Clock**: Ongoing maintenance and seasonal regulation. Quarterly service contract with the parish. A point of pride.
- **Radziwiłł Pocket Watch**: Rare 1780s Polish-Lithuanian pocket watch for the Kraków City Museum. Delicate provenance work alongside Professor Mitchell. Museum deadline December 2026.
- **Personal Junghans wall clock**: Restoring a 1930s piece for the apartment hallway. No deadline.

**Scholarship**: Monograph on Polish longcase clock movements, 1750 to 1850, in collaboration with Professor Mitchell. Slow progress; perhaps two chapters by end of 2026.

**Professional**: Active member of the Kraków Horological Guild. Gave a lecture at the Basel Horology Forum in 2024.

**Long-term goals**: Train Mark to journeyman level by 2027. Build a workshop endowment fund. Complete the Radziwiłł commission for the museum. Plan a slow drift toward semi-retirement.

- **Reading**: Horological history, classical literature (Hemingway, Steinbeck, Thoreau), biographies of craftsmen, occasional Polish history. Reads in English, German, and Polish with varying speeds and full enjoyment.
