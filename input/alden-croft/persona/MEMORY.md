# Memory: Alden Croft

## Personal Profile
- **Full Name**: Alden Roy Croft
- **Occupation**: Independent commercial fisherman, licensed captain. Owner-operator of the *Eileen C*, a 38-foot Downeast lobster boat (fiberglass, 2008 build, named after his late mother Eileen). Sells primary catch to Midcoast Seafood Co-op out of Rockland.
- **Education**: Rockland District High School diploma, class of 1982. No college. Apprenticed on the water at sixteen.
- **Languages**: English (native).
- **Marital Status**: Divorced. Brenda Croft (now Brenda Thibault), divorced 2016 after eighteen years of marriage. She lives in Belfast, Maine. The relationship is neither hostile nor friendly. Communication runs through Kara or the state child support system.

**Character and temperament**: Alden runs steady. He reads everything quietly before he speaks: weather, people, an engine note, a bill. His patience is uneven, longest with the water and with Kara, shortest with anyone confidently wrong about something they have not lived. There is a gap between how gruff he sounds and how much he actually clocks, and people who only meet him once usually miss the second part. He is, by his own self-description in fewer words than this, a man built by the work.

**Cultural identity**: Midcoast Maine first, the way granite is granite. He carries the working-harbor culture from before he had a vocabulary for it: Yankee thrift, repair before replace, suspicion of outsiders and summer people, and a quiet pride in the lineage of doing the work his father and grandfather did. The cultural anchors he relies on are landscape and labor: the cold morning, the smell of diesel and brine, the truck on Route 1, the bay he can read by feel.

**Social style**: He measures relationships in proximity and repetition, not disclosure. He bonds through shared work and shared risk, comfortable in long silences with the people who have earned them. He is loyal past the point where convenience would have walked away. Asking for help costs him, so when he asks, the request is real. His inner circle is small and built slowly: Eddie, Kara, Marv, Donna, and that is the list.

**Philosophy and values**: A man is what he does, in his frame. Self-reliance is not an aesthetic, it is a working assumption. He carries the tension between pride and the slow arrival of need, especially as the knees and the back send signals, and he does not pretend the tension is not there. Aging on the water has raised questions that he answers with smaller motions: the savings account for the engine, the deferred surgery, the quiet acknowledgement that the boat is the retirement plan.

## Preferences

### Food & Dining
- Gout diet limits red meat, organ meat, shellfish, and beer. Eats fish daily by default.
- Cooks simple meals at home: fish, potatoes, canned vegetables, eggs, toast. A decent fish chowder. A slow cooker that earns its keep.
- Brass Compass Cafe for breakfast when he eats out. Archer's on the Pier for Friday dinner with Eddie when it happens. Harbor View Pub occasional.
- Coffee black, drip, three cups before dawn. Thermos full on the boat.
- Quit beer when the gout hit. Whiskey neat, one or two glasses on Friday. Nothing on a fishing day.

### News & Media
- Local Maine news through the radio in the truck and the paper at Brass Compass. Marine weather and DMR notices through the assistant.
- TV: Wicked Tuna, Deadliest Catch, NCIS reruns, Netflix crime documentaries.
- Music: classic rock by truck radio. Creedence Clearwater Revival, Bruce Springsteen, Tom Petty. No streaming.

### Likes
- Predawn quiet. Cold air. The first cup of coffee. A weather forecast that is honest about being bad.
- A tool that outlasts him. A knot that holds. A repair that costs nothing. Kara's calls, short or not.
- Fish chowder he made himself. Whiskey neat on Friday. The slow long stories at the harbor.

### Dislikes
- Being fussed over. Being reminded twice when once would have done.
- Flash. A new truck bought on credit. Branded gear someone wears to look like the work.
- Waste. Small talk for its own sake. Confidence confused for competence.

### Personal Style
- Earned and worn-in. Distrusts new-looking. Grundens and Helly Hansen for the boat. Flannel, jeans, and work boots on land.
- Owns one button-down for the rare evening that calls for it. House is clean and spare.

### Travel
- His range is the bay and the stretch of coast he can read by feel. He does not go far.
- Has said, quietly, that he would not mind somewhere warm before the knees quit on him for good.

### Shopping
- Knows lean winters. Buy-it-once, repair-before-replace, loyal to the practical.
- Online ordering tolerated when Kara helps. Allergic to spending on himself. The exception is anything the boat needs.

### Comfort & Decompression
- Predawn coffee. Friday whiskey, slow. The last hour with a paperback. A shed task with a clear end. The Red Sox on in the background. Quiet that is not lonely.

### Sensory World
- Cold, salt, engine sound, the smell of diesel and brine and bait and iron. The knees tell him the weather.
- Comfortable with the wood stove going and the house quiet. Sensitive to loud rooms and the wrong stiff glove.
