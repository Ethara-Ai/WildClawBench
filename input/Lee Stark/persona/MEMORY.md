# Memory: Lee James Stark

## Personal Profile

Lee grew up on the rural edge of Bozeman, in a house his maternal grandmother built. His mother Carol is a retired schoolteacher with deep Montana roots. His father Harold immigrated from Tromsø, Norway in the 1980s, worked as a carpenter, and returned to Norway after the divorce when Lee was 14. They have rebuilt their relationship in adulthood and now talk every Sunday.

He is a layered American: Montana on his mother's side, Norwegian on his father's. His maternal grandmother's folk remedies and his Norwegian grandmother's folkemedisin shaped him in equal measure, and he has built a herbal practice that draws on both without trying to flatten the threads.

He is a Certified Professional Midwife trained at the Bridgewater Institute for Midwifery Studies (2015) and a clinical herbalist credentialed by the Northern Rockies School of Botanical Arts (2018). He uses pharmaceutical medicine when it is the right tool. He just refuses to grant institutional medicine unquestioned authority, and he has thought carefully about where his skepticism is warranted and where it is not.

He speaks English with a flat Western American cadence and slips into Norwegian when his father is in town or when the Sunday call runs long. His listening Norwegian is stronger than his speaking. He intends to close the gap and has not yet.

He is stubborn under the easygoing surface, emotionally generous to a fault, and bad at extending the same vulnerability inward that he facilitates in clients. He cries privately and rarely. He thinks of the garden, the trail, and the birth room as sacred spaces. He does not call this religion. He does not need to.

He believes birth is a sacred threshold that institutional medicine has too often stripped of its power, and that a midwife restores that power without sacrificing the safety the institutional system can provide. He believes the earth is a relative, not a resource. He would tell Owen, when he is older, that the people who change anything are usually the ones who learned to do the unglamorous work well.

## Work & Projects
- **Bridger Root Midwifery**: Home-birth practice run from the converted barn studio. 4 to 6 active clients at a time, each with about 10 prenatal visits plus birth and postpartum follow-ups. Currently 4 active. Sliding-scale income roughly $3,200/month. Liability insurance due the 15th.
- **Northern Remedy Co.**: Tinctures, salves, teas, and seasonal blends. Year-round line is Fire Cider (a decade-refined recipe) and Elderberry Syrup; the autumn seasonal release is the Autumn Immunity Collection, which adds the Autumn Immune Blend Tincture (elderberry, elderflower, echinacea, astragalus) and the Harvest Tea Blend alongside the year-round pair. Sells at Main Street Saturday Market, Midtown Sunday Market (alternating), and the periodic Gallatin Makers Fair (roughly four to six dates a year). Online shop at northernremedyco.com via WooCommerce; Etsy carries a smaller, ship-friendly subset. Roughly $800 to $1,200/month. Wholesale leads come in occasionally; the most recent is Mountain Meadow Health Foods (Joelle Kessler, buyer) out of the Gallatin Valley, still in early conversation.
- **Cottonwood Basin Organic Co-op**: Co-manager with Donna Whitfield. 14 member families, two seasonal hires via Gusto. Lee handles growing, planting rotation, and equipment days; Donna handles finance and logistics.
- **Apprentice training**: Brett shadows Lee at prenatal visits and births with growing independence. Quarterly formal skills assessments. Target CPM certification 2027.
- **Long arcs**: Six-week Norway trip with the kids planned for a future summer; barn studio expansion to add a small retail space for Northern Remedy Co. (preliminary estimates in hand from Megan); Owen's schooling decisions past second grade.

## Health & Wellness
- Generally excellent health. Walks the walk on the herbalism.
- Daily tinctures: ashwagandha for stress, rhodiola for energy, elderberry in winter.
- Chronic right-shoulder tension from years of catching babies in awkward positions. Massage with Craig Ellison every other Thursday at 2:00 PM.
- Annual wellness check with Dr. Kevin Holbrook at Gallatin Mountain Family Practice. No current medications.
- Runs 15 to 20 miles per week on Bridger Mountain trails. Breathwork and a small grounding ritual before each birth.
- Postpartum hemorrhage during Nora's birth in 2022 left a permanent imprint on how he holds risk in his work.
- Dietary frame: mostly plant-based, eats eggs from their own chickens and occasional locally sourced meat. Drinks yerba mate, not coffee. Avoids processed sugar and most dairy. Daily fermented foods (kraut, kimchi, kombucha). Nora's egg allergy is a hard household constraint.

## Preferences
- **Music**: old-time Americana, folk, bluegrass, indie folk. Tyler Childers, Trampled by Turtles, Iron & Wine.
- **Reading**: ethnobotany, birth narratives, Western literary fiction. Clementine Barrow novels on rotation.
- **Shopping**: local-first. Bulk through the co-op. Avoids big-box stores. Thrift for kids' clothes. Handmade gifts where possible.
- **Aesthetic**: rural Montana with herbalist undertones. Well-worn jeans, soft layered shirts, leather work boots maintained properly. One mildly formal button-down. No suit, by choice. The house is warm chaos; the studio is clinical precision.
- **Travel**: rarely. Drives when possible. Has not flown in years. The deferred trip is six weeks in Norway with the kids.
- **Food and drink**: yerba mate ritual in the first hour of the morning. Comfort foods include Carol's bread, Harold's grandmother's fish soup, fire cider Lee has been refining for a decade, and the kombucha that lives permanently on the counter. Genuinely not a coffee drinker.
- **Sensory**: smell-anchored. The shed in late summer, cold-clean mountain air at dawn, his father's kitchen recreated in his own. Prefers heavy cotton, soil under the fingernails, smooth worn knife handles. Avoids synthetic fabrics and plastic-pretending-to-be-wood. True silence makes him slightly uneasy; the exception is the trail at dawn.
- **Pet peeves**: being called a "lay midwife," herbal medicine dismissed as "woo-woo," plastic bags at farmers markets, drunk drivers, restaurant volume ceilings he finds offensive.
- **Gifts given**: handmade where possible. Salves, jars of preserved cherries, specific winter tinctures. For Megan's birthday, the gesture and the practical object both.
- **Comfort and decompression**: trail first when the day has been heavy, then the wooden stool by the drying racks in the afternoon light. The morning yerba mate ritual is the small ceremony before everything else.
- **Social style**: warmer than the initial reserve suggests, slower to open than the warmth implies. Funny and quietly central in small groups, edge-of-the-room in large ones. Excellent with children, both his own and his clients' families.
