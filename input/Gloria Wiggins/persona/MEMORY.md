# Memory: Gloria Mae Wiggins

## Personal Profile
She is African American, with family roots in the rural Piedmont of North Carolina and deep Gullah/Geechee ties through her grandmother's Lowcountry South Carolina lineage. Her parents, Harold and Denise, raised her in east Durham's historically Black, church-anchored community. She understands more Gullah/Geechee than she speaks fluently, and she has decided the line of language loss stops with her.

Education: Hillside High School, Durham (2017); B.S. in Plant Science from Piedmont Agricultural & Technical University (2021); certificate in Community Health Education from Triangle Institute of Public Health (2023).

Personality: patient, observant, quietly stubborn. She listens before she prescribes and earns trust quickly with the people she serves, while staying selective with those who treat her work as a curiosity. She carries a working anger about the disposability of Black agricultural knowledge in the institutions she trained inside. It shows rarely, but the person on the other side of it remembers.

Philosophy: she holds that the land is a relationship rather than a resource, that Black agricultural knowledge is foundational rather than supplementary, and that cultural recovery is a form of repair. She believes in slow building and has stopped looking for shortcuts.

Faith: her family is anchored at Mount Zion Baptist Church, where her grandfather is buried and where she shows up for the major Sundays. Her own theology is more uncertain than her parents', and she does not make a thing of that.

## Work & Projects
- **Piedmont County Cooperative Extension**: Serves small-scale and Black-owned farms in Durham, Orange, and Chatham counties. Focus areas: drought-resistant varieties, integrated pest management, soil health, and irrigation efficiency.
- **Pay and benefits**: $52,000/year salary, health and dental insurance, state retirement contribution, 15 days PTO.
- **Deep Roots**: Grant application for a culturally responsive agricultural education program integrating traditional Black agrarian knowledge with modern extension science. Budget $85,000 over 2 years.
- **Water rights advocacy**: For 6 small farms affected by a diversion dispute with Piedmont Agri Holdings. She has researched North Carolina water rights precedents for the case.
- **Soil health workshop series**: A recurring program for small-scale farmers, recently launched.
- **Herbalism**: Cultural practice, not a job. Shares preparations and teaches informal classes at Heritage Oak Community Center. Does not charge, treats it as reciprocal community care.
- **Career goal**: Build a replicable model for culturally inclusive agricultural extension.
- Recently compiled a comparison of 5 drought-resistant tomato varieties with yield and water data for Piedmont small farms.

## Interests & Hobbies
- **Herbalism**: Traditional African American root medicine and Southern herbal traditions from Grandma Bernice. Grows lemon balm, mullein, elderberry, echinacea, yarrow, comfrey, and boneset. Makes teas, tinctures, and poultices for community members.
- **Gardening**: Backyard working garden with herbs, vegetables (tomatoes, okra, squash, collard greens), and trial plots for drought-resistant varieties. Helps maintain a community garden at Heritage Oak Community Center.
- **Gullah/Geechee study**: Weekly online class through the Cultural Heritage Corridor, practice with Grandma Bernice, transcription in her field notebook.
- **Cooking**: Southern and soul food. Cooks with her mother on weekends, learning recipes from Grandma Bernice before they are lost.
- **Hiking**: Piedmont trails and Blue Ridge foothills with Tamika, combined with plant identification and wildcrafting.
- **Reading**: Plant science journals, Black agrarian literature, food sovereignty writing, fiction by Black authors.
- **Community organizing**: Volunteers with Heritage Oak Community Center on farmworker health and education.

## Home & Living

- Second bedroom converted to herb processing and storage: drying racks, jars of tinctures and dried herbs, a small work table.
- Large backyard (the reason she chose the house): raised beds, a dedicated medicinal herb section, and a small greenhouse for seedlings.
- 15 minutes to the extension office, 10 minutes to her parents' house, 20 minutes to most field sites.
- Neighborhood is working-class and predominantly Black, near where she grew up.
