# Memory: Ian Salazar

## Personal Profile

Ian is third-generation Mexican-American, Chicano in the considered intentional sense, born and raised in the Mesilla Valley to a family longer in this place than either New Mexico or the United States have been here in their current shapes. His cultural identity is rooted in place as much as in lineage. He moves fluidly between English and Spanish, between his abuela's Spanish-only kitchen and his school's English-only classrooms, between Dia de los Muertos rituals in the family living room and Sunday football in the same room.

He carries the early loss of his father Eduardo, who died in a car accident on Highway 70 in 2017 when Ian was sixteen. The loss sits inside him like a low constant, not a current grief that surfaces daily, but a tectonic plate that shapes the landscape. He measures himself, often unconsciously, against the man Eduardo did not live long enough to fully become.

He is the first generation in his family to finish college and is conscious of the weight of that. His scientific bent and his artisan bent are not in tension. He takes a water sample with the same careful attention he brings to a tooled belt edge. He thinks about a watershed the way he thinks about a leather hide.

His working philosophy is simple: do the work carefully, watch over the place you came from, take care of the people who took care of you, keep your hands honest. He believes in community as a real and earned category. He chose to come home after college against the gravitational pull of higher salaries elsewhere, and he has not regretted it. Faith is present but understated. He grew up Catholic, attends Mass with his mother occasionally, and treats the family altar at Dia de los Muertos with a real and uncynical seriousness.

Education: BS in Environmental Science from Ridgemont State University, Albuquerque, NM (2023, summa cum laude).
Languages: English (native, primary professional language), Spanish (fluent — used with family and in Mesilla Valley fieldwork).

## Work & Projects

Environmental Scientist at Doña Ana County Environmental Services. Reports to Dr. Samuel Olivera. Started Thursday, August 2023, immediately after graduation. Office at the county government complex in Las Cruces, NM. Hours: 8 AM to 5 PM Mon-Fri in office and lab; fieldwork days run 6 AM to 4 PM. Salary $48,000.

Current projects:
- **Water quality monitoring program**: Twelve sites across Doña Ana County, sampled monthly. Tests pH, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, coliform bacteria, heavy metals.
- **Watershed monitoring grant**: Proposal to Clearwater Environmental Trust for a 3-year, $320,000 multi-site monitoring program. Partnering with Dr. Lisa Brennan at Ridgemont State University. Deadline Monday, December 14, 2026.
- **Brownfield assessment**: Former fuel storage site near Hatch. Coordinating with EPA Region 6 and county commissioners.
- **Pronghorn habitat survey (seasonal)**: With Sofia Delgado's NM Game and Fish team. Habitat mapping using GPS data and field observations.

Leatherwork business: Sells through the Etsy shop "Salazar Leathercraft" and at regional fiestas and mercados. Products: leather bracelets and keychains ($35 to $85), tooled leather belts ($120 to $250), custom journal covers and wallets ($200 to $400). Revenue approximately $6,000 to $8,000 per year, growing.

He is a member of professional environmental science organizations relevant to his work in New Mexico and contributes to community-of-practice conversations within state hydrology circles.
