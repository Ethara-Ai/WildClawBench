CUMMINS MARINE — SERVICE BULLETIN
Bulletin No.:   SB-6BTA-2024-07
Date Issued:    2024-07-22
Engine Family:  Cummins 6BTA-5.9 (marine, mid-range)
Subject:        Intermittent overheating under load — diagnostic and corrective actions

Applies to:
  Cummins 6BTA-5.9 marine diesel, all sub-variants, model years 1998–2010.

Symptom:
  Coolant temperature trends 10–20°F above normal operating range under
  sustained load. May trigger overtemp alarm in heavy-load conditions.
  Symptom is intermittent and load-dependent.

Probable causes (in order of likelihood):
  1. Heat exchanger tube fouling (sediment, marine growth, scale).
  2. Thermostat partial-open failure (sticking, debris).
  3. Raw-water impeller wear reducing flow at higher RPM.
  4. Sea-water pump bearing wear.
  5. Engine-coolant pump impeller erosion.

Corrective actions:
  - Inspect and clean heat exchanger tubes; service annually in saltwater.
  - Replace thermostat if cooling response is sluggish on bench test.
  - Replace raw-water impeller at any sign of vane wear.
  - Inspect sea-water pump bearing during haul-out service.

Notes:
  - Do NOT substitute Cummins 6CTA-8.3 parts. The 6CTA cooling components
    are dimensionally similar but flow ratings and thread pitches differ.
    Mixing platforms WILL cause cooling-system failure.

Reference: Cummins Marine Service Manual, Section 5 (Cooling System),
           Chapter 5.4 (Diagnostic Procedures).
