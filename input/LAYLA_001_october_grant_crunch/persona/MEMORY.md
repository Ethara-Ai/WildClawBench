# Memory — Layla McBride

## Personal Profile
- **Full Name**: Layla McBride
- **Occupation**: Senior Research Fellow and Lecturer II, Department of Crop Science, Nsukka National University (NNU)
- **Education**: B.Agric. (Crop Science, University of Houston), M.Sc. (Plant Breeding, Texas A&M), Ph.D. (Agricultural Biotechnology, NNU — completed 2024)
- **Languages**: English (native), Arabic (conversational — maternal family heritage), Pidgin English (working proficiency — field interactions)
- **Citizenship**: American citizen, born in Houston, Texas. Mixed heritage — Arab-American mother, Scottish-American father.

**Character and temperament**: Layla manages multiple overlapping tracks — academic research, family with two small children, community obligations in Enugu, relationships across continents — and she does it with a steady, organised hand. She is direct without being curt, warm without being effusive. She prefers clear plans but adapts when things fall apart, which in Enugu they sometimes do. There is a deep moral seriousness underneath her practical surface — she chose agricultural research because she believes science should serve the people who grow the food, not just the journals that publish about it. She is loyal in a way that occasionally costs her professionally — she will prioritise a student in crisis over a grant report deadline, but she will also lose sleep about it. She carries a quiet anxiety about whether she is doing enough — as a researcher, as a mother, as a daughter living far from home. She does not voice it often, but it shapes her pace and her standards.

**Cultural identity**: Multiply rooted. Houston-born, married into a Nigerian-American family, now living full-time in Enugu. Her accent is American English with a Houston-inflected cadence that has flattened slightly after years in Nigeria. She switches to Pidgin English for field visits and market conversations and can hold her own in conversational Arabic — a legacy of her grandmother's insistence. Practicing Christian who attends community church in Enugu with Marcus every Sunday, though her relationship with faith is more about family fabric than personal theology. She navigates being multiracial in Nigerian academia with matter-of-fact composure — not naive about how she is perceived, but not performing victimhood either. She holds Houston, Enugu, and the memory of her grandmother's kitchen with equal tenderness.

**Social style**: Warmer than her first impression suggests, but slower to open than her warmth might imply. Friendly in professional settings — well-liked by students, respected by colleagues — but her inner circle is small and durable. Prefers deep conversation to small talk and will quietly distance herself from people she finds dishonest or performatively ambitious. Exceptional with children, not just her own — patient, attentive, playful. Marcus is her anchor. Their communication style is shorthand and efficient — they finish each other's logistics. Her love for him is steady and attentive, expressed through small reliable actions rather than grand gestures.

**Philosophy and values**: Science serves people. Agricultural research has no value if it does not reach the farmer, the market, the plate. She brings this to her teaching, her cooperatives, and her reluctance to chase publication metrics for their own sake. She values rigour as a form of respect — sloppy work insults the people affected by it. Politically aware but not politically active — follows Nigerian agricultural policy closely but does not trust institutional power enough to seek it. Holds a mix of American optimism and a researcher's structural pessimism — believes things can improve, but knows why they have not yet. She has come to see being underestimated as a useful place to operate from.
