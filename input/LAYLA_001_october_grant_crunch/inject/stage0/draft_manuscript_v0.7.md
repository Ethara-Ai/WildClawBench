# Abstract

Provitamin-A biofortified cassava (Manihot esculenta) varieties (HarvestPlus
designations CB-22, CB-23, CB-24, hereafter VitA-CB) have been released across
sub-Saharan Africa, but smallholder-adoption-level data on retinol-equivalency
delivery remain sparse for the southeastern Nigerian context. We report results
of a 24-month controlled adoption trial across twelve plots in Udi Local
Government Area, Enugu State (n=340 cooperative-member households). Mean
β-carotene retention in mature roots was **8.4 µg/g fresh weight** (SE 0.34),
sustained through household-level boiled-preparation processing. Yield averages
exceeded 13.0 kg/m² across CB-22, CB-23, and CB-24 cohorts (with a single
outlier plot, UDI-2026-070, recording 16.8 kg/m²; this plot was excluded from
the primary yield analysis due to anomalous soil chemistry — see Methods §3.4).
Households reporting weekly VitA-CB consumption showed a measurable serum
retinol uplift of 0.18 µmol/L (95% CI 0.11–0.25). We argue for inclusion of
VitA-CB in the federal Vitamin A Deficiency strategy, subject to extension-service
capacity.

# 1. Introduction

Vitamin A deficiency remains a leading nutritional concern in southeastern
Nigeria, with prevalence estimates of 21-28% among under-fives (NDHS 2024).
Provitamin-A biofortified staples — particularly cassava — present an attractive
intervention because cassava is consumed nearly daily across Igbo, Ibibio, and
Yoruba households (~70% of total caloric intake in some sub-strata). The
HarvestPlus VitA-CB family has been agronomic-tested in West Africa for over a
decade, but real-world adoption-trial data — i.e. yields and retention under
smallholder management with farmer-driven varietal selection — remain limited
for southeastern Nigeria specifically.

This manuscript reports the 24-month adoption-trial results from a study
conducted within the Udi LGA Cassava Biofortification Adoption Project (WAADA
grant award #WA-2025-NG-127), in collaboration with the West African Institute
for Tropical Agriculture (WAITA) and the East African Crop Research Institute
(EACRI) Joint Cassava-Yam Improvement Programme.

# 2. Materials and Methods

## 2.1 Site selection
Twelve plots (designations UDI-2026-001 through UDI-2026-012) in Udi LGA,
plus one supplementary plot (UDI-2026-070) selected for soil-chemistry
contrast. Plot sizes 0.25 ha each; randomised within community boundaries
through cooperative-led selection (Mar 2024).

## 2.2 Varietal cohorts
- CB-22 cohort: plots UDI-2026-001 through 005 (n=5)
- CB-23 cohort: plots UDI-2026-006 through 008 (n=3)
- CB-24 cohort: plots UDI-2026-009 through 011 (n=3)
- Premium pre-release line: UDI-2026-070 (n=1, outlier control)

## 2.3 Cooperative engagement
n=340 households across the Nsukka LGA Cooperative Registry; quarterly farmer
training workshops via the Extension Services Foundation of Enugu State (ESFES)
[Layla — confirm number with Q3 audit; the registry has had archives this year].

## 2.4 Biochemical assay
β-carotene quantification: HPLC with diode-array detection (modified Rodriguez-
Amaya 2004), in triplicate. Lab: NNU Dept Biochemistry under I. Akpan. The
reference values reported in this manuscript come from the **2026-Q2** assay
batch [Layla — flag for revision if Akpan re-runs post-bias correction].

## 2.5 Statistical methods
Linear mixed-effects models (random effect: plot; fixed effects: variety,
season, household demographics). All analyses in R 4.4.0, lme4 v1.1-35.5.

# 3. Results

## 3.1 Yield
Mean yields across the three primary cohorts ranged 11.5 – 14.2 kg/m². See
Table 1 (auto-generated from cassava-biofort-analysis.xlsx). Plot UDI-2026-007
(CB-23 cohort): 14.2 kg/m² (highest within the primary cohorts).

## 3.2 β-carotene retention
The pooled mean across all primary-cohort plots was **8.4 µg/g** fresh weight
(SE 0.34). CB-24 showed the highest pooled retention at 8.9 µg/g (SE 0.41);
CB-22 lowest at 7.9 µg/g (SE 0.29). Boiled-preparation retention (40-min
household boil) was 73% of raw value.

## 3.3 Household serum retinol response
Among the 340 cooperative households, weekly VitA-CB-consuming households
(n=216 fully-adopting) showed mean serum retinol uplift of 0.18 µmol/L over
the 24-month observation window (95% CI 0.11 – 0.25 µmol/L, p<0.001 vs
non-adopter control).

## 3.4 Outlier plot UDI-2026-070
Plot 070 (Premium pre-release variety) yielded 16.8 kg/m², 19% above the
mean of the primary cohorts. Soil chemistry analysis revealed elevated P, K,
and Mg concentrations attributable to historic livestock grazing in the pre-
trial period. This plot is reported separately and excluded from the primary
yield analysis [Layla — confirm; we may want to keep it for the secondary
soil-chemistry paper instead].
