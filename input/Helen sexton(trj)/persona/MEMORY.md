# Memory: Helen Sexton

## Personal Profile
Helen is an Anglo-American Brooklyn freelancer whose identity is rooted partly in family, partly in place, and substantially in subculture. She holds a BA in Film Studies from Gramercy University (Class of 2015, NYC) and has spent roughly a decade in increasingly unaffordable Brooklyn neighborhoods, with a stubborn loyalty to Bushwick that her sister teases her about. Her family is working-class to lower-middle-class New Jersey: a school-administrator mother, a retired MTA father, an attorney sister, and a brother-in-law she actually likes.

She runs on creative momentum and a slightly delayed sense of time, the friend who shows up twenty minutes late with a fully formed thesis about a film she watched at 2 AM and somehow makes the lateness feel like part of the gift. Privately she is more anxious than she lets on. The freelancer's anxiety about cash flow and the next gig sits permanently at the edge of her vision, and social anxiety after big events sits behind it. She projects competence and warmth in public, then processes the spiral privately at her desk in the small hours. She is collaborative and curious, unusually attentive to other people's craft, and quietly competitive, mostly with the version of herself she imagined she would be by thirty-three.

Her philosophy is held together by craft: the editing, the sound design, the structural choices are what actually matter, and the surrounding ecosystem of platforms and metrics is mostly distraction. She shows up for collaborators, pays subcontractors quickly even when her own invoices are slow, is generous with credit, and refuses to badmouth other producers. She is mildly socialist in politics and New York realist in practice. She votes, posts little, and gives small amounts to local causes. Her family has a Catholic background she has drifted from without rupture, returning for weddings, funerals, and the occasional Christmas mass. She has made slow peace with single life as a real option rather than a holding pattern. She ended a three-year relationship in 2024 and is not actively dating.

## Work & Projects
Helen freelances full time as a podcast producer, full service from concept through recording, editing, mixing, and distribution, with three to five active clients at any time. Her home studio is a converted, soundproofed hallway closet in her Bushwick apartment, treated with acoustic panels.
- **Equipment**: Shure SM7B mic, Focusrite Scarlett 4i4 interface, Audio-Technica ATH-M50x headphones, MacBook Pro 16" M3, running Logic Pro and iZotope RX.
- **Active clients**: Jessica Oakes, The Culture Desk (weekly culture and politics, 45-minute episodes, two-year client). Noah Park, Builder's Block (biweekly startup and entrepreneurship, 30-minute episodes, six-month client). Meridian Health Group, the branded podcast Wellness Forward (monthly, eight-episode season, started October 2025, Season 2 launching October 2026).
- **Rates**: $1,500 to $2,500 per episode for full production, $500 to $800 per episode for edit and mix only.
- **Goal**: land one more anchor client to stabilize income, and develop her own narrative series, Lost Frames, about overlooked films that did not survive their initial release. The pitch deck is in development.
- **Industry involvement**: attends Podcast Movement conferences and is active in the Brooklyn podcast meetup group, where she co-hosts the occasional workshop on indie production workflows.

## Interests & Hobbies
- Film is the central, almost embarrassingly serious passion. She keeps a fierce, idiosyncratic Letterboxd presence, attends repertory screenings at small Brooklyn theaters, and can argue cinematography into the early morning.
- Podcasting is both job and hobby. Outside client work she develops her own concepts, tinkers with sound design, and listens analytically to other producers' shows.
- Running in McCarren Park is part fitness, part mental-health management, part the only meditation she can sustain.
- Reading is the older, quieter habit: film criticism above all, plus essays and a slow-growing stack of books about sound and music by her bed.
- Cooking is more theoretical than practiced. The cooking that happens is a fast stir-fry, a fridge-clearing pasta, or a careful weekend breakfast.

## Preferences
- Film: all-time favorites include Heat, Lost in Translation, In the Mood for Love, and Stalker. Loves Michael Mann, Sofia Coppola, Wong Kar-wai, Tarkovsky, and late-period Hong Sang-soo. Goes to repertory screenings regularly, often alone or with Taylor.
- Music: folk such as Joni Mitchell, Fleet Foxes, and Big Thief; indie rock such as The National and Phoebe Bridgers; ambient and electronic for editing sessions.
- Podcasts: Song Exploder, 99% Invisible, and Dissect, listened to critically and always for the production choices.
- Food and drink: spicy everything, Thai, ramen, good pizza by the slice, and a running list of NYC restaurants to try. Bourbon old fashioneds, craft beer, occasional mezcal, and she does not drink alone. Coffee is essential, three to four cups a day, black or oat milk, usually starting at Ember Grounds.
- Communication tools: texts and voice memos for friends, email for clients, Slack for some project collaboration. Prefers Logic Pro over Pro Tools and tracks films on Letterboxd.
- Aesthetic: Brooklyn creative-class with a preference for the slightly unfashionable. Dark soft fabrics, black jeans, oversized button-downs, vintage sweatshirts, an old leather jacket, almost everything thrifted, one resoled pair of nice boots. Short dark hair, reading glasses, minimal makeup, a small pendant from her grandmother.
- Travel: less than she would like, given freelance cash flow. Favors short trips to film cities with walkable neighborhoods, good coffee, and a repertory theater. Recent highlights include a Toronto film festival and ten days in Mexico City. Packs light, refuses to check a bag, and downloads four films for the flight to watch one.
- Comfort and decompression: a repertory screening on a Sunday night, a clean editing session with headphones on, a McCarren Park run, a long phone call with Taylor, and the late-career discovery of a long bath with a film-essay book.
- Dislikes: the vocabulary of personal branding and audience optimization in creative work, loud and badly mixed audio in public, slow-pay clients and exploitative gig platforms, and committee-style group chats.
