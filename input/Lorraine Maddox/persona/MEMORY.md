# Memory: Lorraine Maddox

## Personal Profile

Born in Bristol, England, to a West Country father and a London mother. Moved to Porto at eighteen for university and has not left. British by birth, Portuguese by daily practice; the Britishness shows in her humour, her tea, and her reading, and the Portuguese-ness shows in her speech rhythms, her mealtime sense, and her relationship to the city itself.

Education: Licenciatura in Urban and Regional Planning, FEUP (2013). M.Sc. in Sustainable Urban Design, Nordlund Institute of Design, Sweden (2015). Erasmus exchange at Delft Institute of Urbanism. Languages: English (native), Portuguese (fluent, slight British accent), French (fluent), Swedish (conversational, from Lund years), basic Dutch (from Delft).

Religion: raised loosely Anglican, has internalised the ethical content without continuing the observance. Closest thing to a sacred practice is the long Sunday walk along the Douro.

Personality: thinks before she speaks. Direct without being brusque, warm without being effusive. Reads other people's body language with the same attention she reads a streetscape. Carries a slow-burning frustration with the political compromises her work requires and manages it with walks, sketching, and long conversations with Brian. Loyal in ways that have occasionally cost her professionally; has refused private-sector job offers because the work needs to happen inside the municipal system.

Philosophy: good planning is invisible. Streets that feel safe, parks that draw families, transit that just works are the discipline's highest expression. Public participation is a design input, not a regulatory checkbox. Holds heritage and progressive instincts in deliberate tension; the planners who resolve that tension cleanly in either direction usually produce worse work.

## Work & Projects

Employer: Câmara Municipal do Porto, Department of Urban Planning and Heritage. Senior Planner. Also runs EU-funded consulting engagements with departmental approval. Tenure: ten years in the discipline, current senior role at the Câmara since 2021.

Focus areas: urban regeneration, public space design, heritage-sensitive densification, and climate adaptation. Schedule: office Monday to Friday, 9 AM to 6 PM; site visits 2 to 3 times per week; community consultation evenings as scheduled; consulting work on personal time with department approval.

Current projects:

- **Campanhã Riverfront Regeneration**. Lead planner. Transforming a former industrial waterfront into mixed-use public space with green corridors, affordable housing, and transit integration. - **Cedofeita Pedestrian Zone Expansion**. Designing an expansion of the pedestrian network connecting the existing commercial corridor to Jardim do Marquês. Traffic impact study underway. Stakeholder consultations with businesses and residents in progress.
- **EU Green Corridors Grant Application**. Cross-border application with Vigo, Spain, for EU Horizon funding to develop green infrastructure corridors linking the two cities. Application due October 2, 2026.

Professional development: invited to present at the European Urban Planning Congress, Brussels, November 9 to 11, 2026 (presenting on Day 2). Considering a short course in climate-adaptive urban design at the Zurich Institute of Urban Futures in 2027.

## Interests & Hobbies

- **Urban sketching**: sketchbook in her bag, drawings made at cafés, on benches, at long meetings. The sketchbook from each year is an unintentional record of how Porto has shifted; the drawings are how she thinks.
- **Walking**: to work, on project sites, through the city for thinking. She has worked out more design problems on the Douro riverfront than at her desk. Strong opinions about which Porto streets are walkable in which seasons.
- **Reading**: urban planning theory at the desk, English-language literary fiction on the bedside table, architecture monographs on the coffee table. Gehl's *Cities for People* is a touchstone. She reads slowly and underlines in pencil.
- **Running**: three mornings a week along the Douro at a comfortable pace, one direction, podcast in ears.
- **Azulejo photo essay**: a near two-year personal project documenting Porto's vanishing tiled facades. Close to six hundred images so far. Not in a hurry to publish; the slow accumulation is the project.
- **Sunday walks**: long, unhurried, often through a neighbourhood she is studying.
